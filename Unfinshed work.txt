Unfinished work
================

1. Source-verify and expand all 100 fable texts
   All 100 catalogue entries currently resolve to five story pages, but the
   existing page text has not been checked against a traceable primary text or
   reliable historical edition for every tale. No extra historical detail was
   added where a source could not be verified. Complete this only after
   recording an appropriate source for each story; do not invent episodes or
   present uncertain retellings as documented history.

2. Translate complete story narratives
   The catalogue interface and all 100 fable titles now have Portuguese,
   English, German, and French wording. The five-page narrative text itself is
   still Portuguese. When another language is selected, the story reader now
   explicitly identifies that Portuguese fallback. Source-checked translations
   for each supported language remain outstanding; translating unverified text
   would preserve and spread possible historical errors.

3. City-content localization - DONE 2026-10-21
   Every tale title, city name, landmark line and costume that was still
   falling back to Portuguese now has en-GB, de-DE and fr-FR wording:
   772 tale titles, 573 city names, 573 landmark lines and 168 costumes per
   locale, in src/data/i18n-country.json. src/i18n/content.ts reads it through
   COUNTRY_CONTENT before falling back to the older TALES and OUTFITS tables,
   and translateCity() now takes the iso so it can look the entry up.
   Spot-checked in the running app: North Korea reads "Costume: pink and white
   hanbok", "The Frog Who Did the Opposite", "A Village of Pines" in English,
   with the German and French data verified separately. What was left alone:
   story bodies (item 2), the Top 100 catalogue copy, and the fallback strings
   that already live in messages.ts.

4. Decide whether to use a measurable ranking for the Top 100
   The catalogue now uses an approximate editorial order that puts broadly
   familiar stories first, and it explicitly says there is no definitive
   worldwide ranking. No globally representative popularity dataset was
   verified, so this order is not a statistical ranking.

5. Finish city photos (touristic-first policy, visual acceptance)
   Policy set 2026-10-21: a photo must show a recognised tourist place of that
   city; aerial/drone framing is preferred but not required when the landmark
   is only photographable from the ground. Licence must be free for commercial
   use and credited, and every image is checked by eye before it ships.

   Review of 2026-10-21 replaced two photos that had passed the old metadata
   filter but showed the wrong subject:
   - Melbourne: was an aerial panorama of Box Hill (a suburb); now Northbank
     and the Yarra, by Dietmar Rabich, CC BY-SA 4.0.
   - Baghdad: was a 5:1 Tigris strip (2710x542, US military frame); now an
     aerial of the Tigris bend, public domain (US DoD photo).
   It also filled seven of the eight gaps reported on 2026-10-20:
   - Mexico City: Palacio de Bellas Artes over the historic centre, Diego
     Delso, CC BY-SA 4.0.
   - Lake Yojoa: sunset over the lake, Suiseisekiryu, CC0 (moody; replace if a
     brighter daylight panorama turns up).
   - Madrid: Plaza Mayor, Matt Joseph, CC BY-SA 4.0.
   - Poznań: Old Market Square and Town Hall from above, Lesiex, CC BY-SA 4.0.
   - Tirana: Skanderbeg Square, Chris Walts, CC BY-SA 2.0.
   - Ohrid: Church of St John at Kaneo, Tahir1434, CC BY-SA 4.0.
   - Tunis: Zitouna Mosque and the medina, Ben guedria houcine, CC BY-SA 4.0.

   Port-au-Prince remains blank on purpose: the only modern photographs on
   Commons show earthquake damage (National Palace, cathedral ruins) and are
   unsuitable for a children's app. Leave it blank rather than use a ruined
   building or a low-quality market snapshot.

   Coverage after the 2026-10-21 bulk pass: 328 of 573 city slots have a
   photo (245 blank). That pass ran scripts/source-city-photos.mjs over every
   blank slot, built contact sheets, reviewed all 293 shortlisted slots by eye
   (115 accepted, 178 rejected) and shipped the accepted ones with
   scripts/apply-city-photos.mjs. Rejections were mostly wrong-place matches
   (Belém, Brazil for Bethlehem; Bologna for Bo; a Japanese onsen for Lake
   Togo; MOCA Cleveland for Moca), archival or satellite frames, plane windows
   and anonymous sprawl. 67 slots returned no candidate at all: small and
   remote places with thin Commons coverage.

   What remains: a second pass over the 178 rejected slots using candidates
   two and three, which the first pass never showed; and the 67 empty ones
   need a different source or stay blank. scripts/fetch-city-photos.mjs
   accepts candidates on filename metadata alone - it cannot see a plane
   window, a suburb, or a 5:1 panorama - so its "ok" lines are a shortlist,
   not a verdict. Keep the visual check. Its 1600px minimum is below the
   app's 4K/Full HD expectation, and some older files remain under 1920px.

6. Roll dish photos out to the other countries
   Brazil shipped 2026-10-21 with three dishes: feijoada, pão de queijo and
   brigadeiro. Policy, matching the city photos: the photo shows the dish
   itself, with nothing else competing; a dish served on a plate is
   photographed on a plate, and hand food (bread, sweets) is left as it is
   served instead of being forced onto one. The licence must allow commercial
   reuse, and the credit sits under the photo and in the full-size view. Every
   image is checked by eye: scripts/fetch-dish-photos.mjs only shortlists
   candidates, it cannot see a plate of the wrong dish.

   Rejected while choosing Brazil's three: a golden basket of pão de queijo
   that carried a TudoGostoso watermark, a clean plated feijoada that was only
   1080px wide, and the Portuguese Wikipedia feijoada still life because a
   cachaça bottle and glass sat in the frame. The three that shipped come from
   Wikimedia Commons (CC0, CC BY 2.0, CC BY-SA 2.0) at 1920px.

   Dish names and the one easy-read line per dish (readers of eight to ten) in
   all four locales live in src/data/dishes.ts. The section renders only where
   a country has dish data, so unreleased countries show nothing rather than a
   placeholder.

   The Americas shipped on 2026-10-21 in three waves, 20 countries and 52
   dishes: Brazil, then South America (Argentina, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia,
   Ecuador, Peru, Uruguay, Venezuela, Paraguay), then Central America (Belize,
   Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, Panama), then North
   America (Canada, Mexico, United States). Countries now carry two or three
   dishes depending on what passed the eye check; the section hint no longer
   promises three.

   Left blank on purpose: Guyana and Suriname. Commons had one dark bowl of
   pepperpot with a fork in shot and a rolling-pin snapshot of roti, and
   nothing for cook-up rice or pom. Guyana's only plausible roti frame carried
   a hand and a paper towel. Blank beats a wrong or ugly plate.

   Second dishes were dropped rather than guessed: Paraguay lost vori-vori
   (the search returns a handball player named Igor Vori) and mbeyú (grey,
   flat photographs); El Salvador lost tamal de elote to a dessert shot;
   Guatemala's chuchitos matched a Mexico City restaurant called Chuchito
   Pérez; Panama's ropa vieja and sancocho matched Cuban and Canary Islands
   pots; Canada dropped tourtière because every candidate had a hockey flag
   baked into the pie or a wine glass in frame.

   Rejected while choosing Brazil's three: a golden basket of pão de queijo
   that carried a TudoGostoso watermark, a clean plated feijoada that was only
   1080px wide, and the Portuguese Wikipedia feijoada still life because a
   cachaça bottle and glass sat in the frame.

   How the pipeline works now: dish definitions live in
   scripts/dish-sources.json (slug + Commons search phrases), the confirmed
   Commons titles live in scripts/dish-picks.json, and
   scripts/fetch-dish-photos.mjs --candidates/--apply does the fetching with
   scripts/dish-candidates-sheet.ps1 building the contact sheet for the eye
   check. New waves need no code change: add the slugs to dish-sources.json,
   review, record the picks, apply, then write the names and notes in
   src/data/dishes.ts.

   Remaining: the rest of the world. The Central American plates are the
   weakest of the Americas (home-style snaps, paper plates, a salt sachet in
   the taco frame) and are the first candidates for a second pass with
   candidates three and four.

   Europe shipped on 2026-10-21 as a parallel wave. Four workers each owned a
   disjoint set of countries and wrote a fragment per country; the fragments
   are merged by scripts/merge-dish-fragments.mjs into src/data/dish-copy.json
   (name/note/plated for the newer waves), scripts/dish-picks.json and
   scripts/dish-sources.json. Countries with dishes so far: Portugal, Spain,
   France, Italy, Greece, Malta, Cyprus, Andorra, San Marino, Germany, Austria,
   Switzerland, Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Denmark, Sweden, Norway,
   Finland, Iceland, Ireland, United Kingdom, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia,
   Hungary, Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia, Serbia, Montenegro, North Macedonia,
   Albania, Kosovo, Romania, Bulgaria, Russia, Lithuania, Latvia, Ukraine.

   Blank on purpose: Monaco (barbagiuan has no licence-passing photo; socca
   candidates show a cook's hands, a menu or the pan), Vatican City (no
   national cuisine), Moldova (candidates duplicated the Romanian photos or
   were cluttered home shots), Belarus (draniki always with cutlery roll or
   beer; machanka had a plastic bag in frame), Estonia (kohuke half-eaten or
   in a hand; verivorst a dim bowl).

   Quality notes for a later pass: Switzerland carries two dishes because
   raclette has no presentable photograph (a smear of cheese on a plate); the
   Swiss fondue shot is a pot with forks, not a plated dish. Several small
   countries have one or two dishes only.

   Worker hint: they wrote fragments through PowerShell, which double-encodes
   UTF-8. The merger now repairs CP1252 mojibake on read, so accents and
   Commons titles survive; do not remove that repair.

   Blank on purpose, all for thin Commons coverage of plated food: Botswana,

   Africa is the weakest wave visually: Southern Africa usually has one usable

   Two data gaps found and fixed while shipping: the country name table was

   Seychelles, which made those countries unsearchable and their dishes
   unreachable. They are in world-meta.json names now.

   Remaining ideas: a second pass over the weak African plates and over the
