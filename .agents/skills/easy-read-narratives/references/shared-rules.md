# Shared narrative rules

## Reader and voice

The reader is 8 to 10 years old, often reading aloud with an adult. The narrator is a friendly guide from the country, already named in the country panel. Keep that voice: warm, calm, concrete. Do not talk down to the child and do not explain the moral twice.

## Page rhythm

A tale has five pages. Each page is a beat:

1. Meet the character and the place.
2. The ordinary world, or the rule that will be broken.
3. The turn: the problem, the warning, the strangeness.
4. What the character does about it.
5. What remains: the custom, the lesson, the echo.

Do not add pages. Do not move a beat to another page unless the Portuguese source already does.

## Sentences

- Aim 8 to 14 words. Ceiling 20. Count when unsure.
- One subject and one action per sentence. Join only ideas that belong together, with "and", "because", "so", "but".
- Put the actor first. "The girl threw the sieve", not "The sieve was thrown by the girl".
- Do not stack relative clauses ("the man who had the dog that barked"). Start a new sentence.
- Questions only when the source asks one.

## Words

- Prefer the everyday word: "use" not "utilise", "help" not "facilitate", "big" not "substantial".
- Repeat a word rather than hunting for a synonym. Children need the repetition.
- Explain a culture word once, inside the sentence: "the sertão, the dry backlands of Brazil, ...".
- Keep numbers concrete: "seven years", not "a period of seven years".
- Avoid words the source does not support: no "magical", "mystical", "ancient curse" unless the Portuguese says so.

## What must not change

- Names of people, places, animals.
- The sequence of events.
- Who does what.
- Cultural details: clothing, food, instruments, beliefs.
- The ending, including a sad or open ending.

## Read-aloud test

Read the page aloud at a normal pace. If you run out of breath mid-sentence, split it. If you stumble on a word, replace it. If a listener asks "who?", the sentence is ambiguous.

## Worked example (pt-BR to en-GB)

Source: "No interior do Brasil, quando o vento junta as folhas num redemoinho, o povo da roça diz que o Saci-Pererê está chegando."

Draft: "In the countryside of Brazil, when the wind lifts the leaves into a whirlwind, people say that Saci-Pererê is coming."

Why: 22 words is close to the ceiling; the clause "when the wind lifts the leaves into a whirlwind" is one clear image. Do not shorten it into "when leaves spin" because the whirlwind is the point of the tale.

## Worked example (source to de-DE)

Source: "Ele é um menino de uma perna só, pele escura, gorro vermelho e um cachimbo na boca."

Draft: "Er ist ein Junge mit nur einem Bein. Seine Haut ist dunkel, er trägt eine rote Mütze und hat eine Pfeife im Mund."

Why: one idea per sentence, present tense, no genitive, no relative clause.
